# Campaign launch

Every great product starts with a promise you can say out loud.

This campaign is built around three moments: the tease, the reveal, and the follow-through. Each one gets its own week, its own assets, and its own measurable goal. We don't move to the next moment until the current one has done its job.

The tease week is about curiosity. Short clips, no product shots, no logo until the last frame. We want people asking what this is, not being told what it is. Success looks like shares and saves, not clicks.

Every great product starts with a promise you can say out loud.

The reveal week is the opposite. Full product, full story, full pricing, all on day one. Nothing held back, because held-back information reads as distrust. The landing page goes live at 9am eastern, the video drops an hour later, and the founders spend the afternoon answering questions in public.

The follow-through week is where most campaigns die, and it's where ours will be won. Real customers, real workflows, real numbers on screen. No testimonials written by us and approved by them — actual footage of the product earning its keep.

Budget is split forty, forty, twenty across the three weeks. If the tease underperforms we shift spend forward, never backward. A weak tease with a strong reveal is a campaign; a strong tease with a weak reveal is an apology.

Every great product starts with a promise you can say out loud. That's the line we open with, the line we close with, and the standard every asset in this campaign has to meet before it ships.
